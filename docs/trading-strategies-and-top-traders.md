# Every Viral Strategy Repackages One Liquidity Trade

Nearly every strategy now sold to retail traders for Forex, Nasdaq and S&P 500 futures and gold, including ICT, Smart Money Concepts (SMC), Candle Range Theory (CRT), TJR's model, the TTrades Fractal Model and Quarterly Theory, is the same five-step trade with a different name on it: mark a pool of resting stop orders, wait for price to sweep it, wait for a sharp shift in short-term structure, enter on the pullback into a fair value gap or order block, and target the opposite pool. The idea underneath is old and partly real, since a New York Fed study of a dealing bank's order book found that stop-losses cluster just beyond round numbers and speed up moves when triggered ([FRBNY Staff Report 125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.html)), and Linda Raschke and Laurence Connors published the fade-the-failed-breakout setup as "Turtle Soup" in 1995 ([TurtleTrader](https://www.turtletrader.com/trader-raschke/)); **but this research found no independent backtest or peer-reviewed study showing that ICT, SMC, CRT or TJR setups are profitable**, vendors who teach them concede that no dependable hit rate has been published ([LiquidityScan](https://liquidityscan.io/blog/ict-weekly-profiles-explained-daily-behavioral-templates)), and ICT's promised 2024 Robbins World Cup statements never appeared ([ICT on X](https://x.com/I_Am_The_ICT/status/1717230023906939175)). The traders with the strongest verified or semi-verified records (Paul Tudor Jones, George Soros and Stanley Druckenmiller, Ed Seykota, the Turtles, four-time World Cup champion Andrea Unger, 2023 champion Ivan Scherman and Peter Brandt) made their money with macro conviction, systematic trend following, short-term breakout and mean-reversion systems and classical chart patterns, all with strict risk sizing, and **none of them is documented using ICT or SMC**. The best academic evidence instead favours slow, diversified trend following ([Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463)), flow-driven timing effects around FX benchmark fixes ([Krohn, Mueller & Whelan](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13306)) and into the equity close ([Gao et al.](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)), and reactions at widely watched round-number levels ([Osler](https://resources.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)), while retail base rates are grim: **roughly 70–89% of retail CFD accounts lose money** ([The Investors Centre](https://www.theinvestorscentre.co.uk/trading/statistics/cfd-trading/); [ZeroToInvest](https://zerotoinvest.com/en/reality-check/cfd-verlustquoten/)), **97% of Brazilian index-futures day traders who persisted beyond 300 days lost money** ([FGV](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b/full)), and only **about 7% of prop-challenge buyers ever receive a payout** ([QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/prop-firm-statistics)). "TGRs" almost certainly means TJR (Tyler J. Riches); **"ACRAM" matched no known strategy, educator or acronym**, the most likely candidate is a CRT-based term such as "CRT + AMD", and the report asks where you heard it. What follows catalogues well over a hundred concepts by family (how to identify each, entry, stop, target, session and best market), profiles nearly 40 traders, and grades the evidence for each family.

*This is educational material, not financial advice. Leveraged trading carries a high risk of losing money.*

## Smart-money models all run one five-step liquidity trade

The "smart money" family descends from Michael J. Huddleston, who teaches as "ICT" (the Inner Circle Trader). Sources describe SMC as a community-built simplification of his material. One prop-firm explainer calls ICT a specific branded methodology and SMC "the broader, community-developed term for institutional price-action trading" ([Phidias](https://phidiaspropfirm.com/education/ict-vs-smc); [AlgoKings](https://www.algokings.net/learn/smc-vs-ict/)). ICT adds **time** to price: killzones, Power of Three, market-maker models and fixed entry windows. Generic SMC mostly drops these ([Quantum-Algo](https://www.quantum-algo.com/blog/smc-vs-ict-concepts/)). The "smart money" idea is much older than either. It goes back to Richard Wyckoff's early-1900s "Composite Man" ([TradingWyckoff](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/smart-money-concepts/)).

ICT's teaching came in two eras: a **2016–17 paid mentorship** focused on Forex, and **free lessons on YouTube and X from 2022** focused on index futures, beginning with the free 2022 mentorship ([Quantum-Algo](https://www.quantum-algo.com/blog/guides/ict-2022-model-complete-guide/)), continuing through a 2024 lecture series that introduced an "8:30 AM model" ([innercircletrader.net](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-2024/lecture-1/)), and reaching the "Venom" model, announced on ICT's own X account in April 2025 ([ICT on X](https://x.com/I_Am_The_ICT/status/1907980579598954555)). **No new ICT-named model from late 2025 or 2026 was found.**

Every named ICT model reviewed (the 2022 model, Silver Bullet, Unicorn, Market Maker models, Judas swing and Power of Three, Venom and Turtle Soup) shares one skeleton: (1) identify a liquidity pool, (2) see it raided, (3) see a displacement or market-structure shift, (4) enter on a retrace into a "PD array" such as an FVG, order block or breaker, and (5) target the opposing pool ([innercircletrader.net](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/complete-ict-trading-strategy-2022/); [AlgoKings](https://www.algokings.net/learn/ict-2022-model/)). One educator states the community's main filter: "price has to break the most recent counter-trend swing on the lower timeframe before I act" ([forexmt4systems](https://forexmt4systems.com/education/smart-money-concepts/ict-2022-model/)).

The microstructure behind the "sweep" is the best-supported part of the whole framework. Osler's order-book data showed take-profit orders clustering *at* round numbers and stop-losses clustering *just beyond* them. Stop-loss execution moves price more than take-profit execution does, which produces "price cascades" ([FRBNY SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.html); [FRBNY SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.html)). That evidence is about round numbers in 1990s FX data. It is not about the specific equal highs, trendlines or session extremes that ICT traders mark.

### PD arrays are the zones ICT expects price to revisit

"PD arrays" (premium/discount arrays) are ICT's catalogue of zones that price is expected to return to. They come in two groups: **imbalances** (gaps, voids and opening gaps) and **blocks** (order, breaker, mitigation, rejection and propulsion blocks). The core rule is to buy bullish arrays only in the **discount** half of the dealing range and sell bearish arrays only in the **premium** half: "Bullish PD Arrays only work in the discount zone" ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/pd-arrays/)).

The core definitions are consistent across sources: FVG, BISI/SIBI, consequent encroachment, BPR, NWOG and order block. The definitions of mitigation block, vacuum block, volume imbalance and NDOG timing are not. Sources also give conflicting priority rankings for the arrays.

### Displacement and CISD decide when to pull the trigger

ICT's word for a trend change is the **market structure shift (MSS)**: a break of the most recent short-term swing after a liquidity raid, made with displacement. Displacement means an energetic move of large-bodied candles that leaves at least one FVG.† The 2022 model looks for this on the 5-, 3- or 1-minute chart ([Quantum-Algo](https://www.quantum-algo.com/blog/guides/ict-2022-model-complete-guide/)).

A finer trigger became prominent around 2023–24: the **change in state of delivery (CISD)**. It is "a body close through the opening price of the final consecutive up- or down-closing candles into the extreme" ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/change-in-state-of-delivery/)). A valid CISD is said to need a prior sweep of a major high or low, or a tap of an HTF point of interest ([TradingFinder](https://tradingfinder.com/education/forex/ict-cisd-trading-style/)). It fires "often before a full ChoCh is confirmed" ([TradingFinder](https://tradingfinder.com/education/forex/mss-vs-cisd/)).

In practice the two form a two-speed confirmation system: **CISD** gives earlier entries with tighter stops but more false signals, while **MSS** confirms later with wider stops.

ICT's swing hierarchy defines which breaks count.† A short-term high is any three-candle swing high. An intermediate-term high is a short-term high with lower short-term highs on both sides. A long-term high is an intermediate-term high flanked by lower intermediate-term highs. ICT does not use "break of structure" or "change of character." Those labels come from SMC.†

Above the intraday clock, ICT layers longer-horizon templates.

**IPDA lookbacks.** The 20/40/60-trading-day ranges define the dealing range. They "shift" quarterly, but sources disagree on when: at calendar quarter boundaries, "every 3 to 4 months," or when price closes beyond the 60-day extreme ([innercircletrader.net](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-ipda/); [Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/ict-dealing-range-ipda-data-range-guide)).

**Weekly profiles.** One community catalogue lists 12 weekly patterns, such as the **classic Tuesday low of the week** (Monday manipulates, then Tuesday drops into an HTF discount array and sets the weekly low) and the **Wednesday reversal** (Monday–Tuesday consolidate, then Wednesday runs sell stops and reverses). One analysis puts it bluntly: "There is no dependable published hit rate" ([innercircletrader.net](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-weekly-range-profiles/); [LiquidityScan](https://liquidityscan.io/blog/ict-weekly-profiles-explained-daily-behavioral-templates)).

**Seasonal tendencies.** In the 2016–17 mentorship, ICT taught month-by-month seasonal tendencies for the dollar, the majors and commodities.†

**Daily bias.** ICT's routine runs in four steps:† find the HTF draw on liquidity; note whether the previous day's high or low was swept and whether price closed back inside it (favouring a reversal) or beyond it (favouring continuation); locate price relative to the midnight open; and use the weekly profile to anticipate which day sets the week's high or low.

### Power of Three, market-maker models and SMT supply the story

**Power of Three (PO3), also called AMD, maps a session or candle onto accumulation, manipulation and distribution.** In the session version, accumulation is the Asian consolidation, manipulation is a London-open false move that runs stops, and distribution is the true move in New York ([theinnercircletraders.com](https://www.theinnercircletraders.com/ict-power-of-3/); [innercircletrader.net](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-power-of-3/)). On a single candle, a bullish day opens, drops below the open to form its low, expands to the high and closes near it. The rule is to buy below the opening price on bullish days.† The template is fractal, which is why sources disagree about when the Judas swing happens: some describe the daily candle and others the session.

**The Market Maker Buy and Sell Models (MMBM/MMSM, together "MMXM") stretch the same logic over several legs.** One community sequence for the buy model runs: accumulate → Judas swing down that sweeps sell-side liquidity → rally to the buy-side target ([theinnercircletraders.com](https://www.theinnercircletraders.com/ict-market-maker-buy-sell-model/)). Stage counts conflict. Sources describe four, five or six stages ([Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/blog/ict-market-maker-model-mmxm)). The five-stage version, the closest match to the unretrieved 2016–17 slide, runs:† an original consolidation at a premium, a sell-side curve of lower highs, a "smart money reversal" at an HTF discount array (often with SMT divergence), a buy-side curve mirroring the sell side, and completion back at the original consolidation, which is the target.

**SMT divergence is the confirmation layer.** It compares two correlated markets at a liquidity raid: a **bearish SMT** is one market making a higher high while its partner does not, and a **bullish SMT** is one making a lower low while its partner holds ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/smart-money-technique-divergence/)). The standard pairs are ES vs. NQ for indices, EURUSD vs. GBPUSD for Forex, gold vs. silver for metals, and EURUSD vs. the dollar index (DXY) as an inverted pair. A "triple SMT" adds a third market such as YM ([fxnx](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/ict-triple-smt-divergence-spot-smart-money)). Vendors themselves warn that "correlations can drift" ([Quantum-Algo](https://www.quantum-algo.com/blog/guides/smt-divergence-complete-guide/)). Futures data bears this out. **YM/ES is the most tightly correlated index pair, not NQ/ES.** RTY/NQ is the weakest at 0.7482, and it fell to 0.6160 in 2024 ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/data/intermarket-correlation-data)).

ICT also uses Treasury bond futures and the dollar index as intermarket context, especially the dollar for gold. His specific bond rules were not documented this session.†

### Candle Range Theory turns every candle into a range

CRT, originally "Candles Are Ranges Theory," is widely credited to the X/Telegram trader **@Romeotpt ("Raid")**. Authorship is debated, and the space is "dense with impersonators and course resellers trading on his name" ([CRT Terminal](https://crtterminal.com/learn/who-created-crt); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/candle-range-theory/); [Sham on X](https://twitter.com/SpeculatorFL/status/1784493604473618604)).

**The core rule:** take a reference ("CRT") candle whose high and low contain a recent block of price. The next candle must sweep **only one side** and close back **inside**. That sweep is the manipulation. The target is the **opposite end** of the range, and price "often provides multiple 'turtle soup' entry opportunities" on the way ([TradingView "Ighodalo Gold CRT"](https://www.tradingview.com/script/UEPdjqLf-Ighodalo-Gold-CRT-Candles-are-ranges-theory/)). A close on or beyond the swept side counts as a breakout, not a CRT ([ProEA](https://proea.app/en/blog/candle-range-theory)). Stricter versions grade the rejection by whether the close or the wick reaches the reference range's 50% level ([TradingView CRT](https://ar.tradingview.com/script/ZSDolrKY-CRT-Candle-Range-Theory)).

**The three-candle framing is PO3 at candle level.** Candle 1 is the range (accumulation), candle 2 is the sweep ("turtle soup"), and candle 3 is the expansion; "The Power of Three (PO3) is 3 candles CRT" ([Scribd CRT overview](https://www.scribd.com/document/888159150/Candle-Range-Theory-CRT)). One deck describes entry models on the second candle (the turtle soup, with an OB or "CSD" confirmation) and on the third candle, relative to its open and to the 50% of candle 2 ([Legacy Trading](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/candle-range-theory-crt-by-legacy-trading-pdf/283734795)).

**The viral version is the time-anchored "4H CRT."** It uses the four-hour candles that open at **1 a.m., 5 a.m. and 9 a.m. New York**. The 1 a.m. candle is the range, and the 5 a.m. candle must take its high or low. The trader then waits for a **15-minute market-structure shift**. That script's author calls it "particularly effective" on gold, EUR/USD, NAS100, US30 and the S&P 500, which is an unverified claim ([TradingView 4H CRT](https://www.tradingview.com/script/kXR0vkG2-4H-CRT-1AM-and-5AM)).

**As commonly taught, a full CRT trade** marks the HTF candle (Daily, 4H or 1H); waits for a one-sided sweep and close back inside, ideally into a key level such as PDH/PDL or an HTF order block or FVG; drops to the paired lower timeframe (Daily→1H/15m, 4H→15m/5m, 1H→5m/1m); waits for an MSS or CISD; enters at the lower-timeframe FVG or OB with the stop beyond the sweep wick; and takes partial profit at the range's 50% and the rest at the opposite extreme. MT5 scanners and expert advisors (EAs) now sell this logic with vendor-only performance claims ([MQL5 CRT Scanner](https://www.mql5.com/fr/market/product/183976); [MQL5 RA CRT Engine](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/160677)). **Critics note that the widely shared 1 a.m./5 a.m. rule "does not define a stop, a target or a time zone."** They also note that no published evidence shows it works on 4H candles ([ProEA](https://proea.app/en/blog/candle-range-theory); [TradingWyckoff](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/crt/)).

Several community sub-terms could not be sourced: "CRT + Key Level," "Purge/Reclaim," "Inside bar CRT," and "SMT with CRT," which checks whether a correlated market failed to sweep its matching level at candle 2.

### No independent test supports the smart-money family

**This research found no peer-reviewed or independent statistical validation of any ICT, SMC, CRT or TJR concept or model.** The family's own educators concede the gap: the 2022 model has no "independent performance data" and its tutorials are "not backtested evidence" ([Quantum-Algo](https://www.quantum-algo.com/blog/guides/ict-2022-model-complete-guide/)); weekly profiles are "a template, not a statistic" ([LiquidityScan](https://liquidityscan.io/blog/ict-weekly-profiles-explained-daily-behavioral-templates)); no source shows that macro windows are algorithmic in any verifiable sense, and their minutes "vary between sources and course years" ([strefatradingu](https://strefatradingu.pl/en/blog/ict-macro-times/)); and CRT sources "are not peer-reviewed," with "none of them" being "proof that the setups work as advertised" ([Scribd CRT](https://www.scribd.com/document/888159150/Candle-Range-Theory-CRT)).

The deeper problem is that the definitions keep changing. The mitigation block, vacuum block, NDOG timing, killzone and macro minutes, and the ranking of PD arrays all differ from source to source ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/mitigation-block/) vs. [innercircletrader.net](https://innercircletrader.net/tutorials/ict-mitigation-block-explained/)). Combined with discretionary rules, this makes the methods hard to falsify: two practitioners can label the same chart differently after the fact. **That is not the same as proof that the setups lack edge.** It means the claimed edge is untested. The closest documented antecedents are older ideas covered in the next section: Osler's tested stop-cascade evidence, plus Raschke's Turtle Soup and Wyckoff's spring, which are well specified but themselves untested.

## Round numbers and reversal bottoms hold up; Fibonacci and harmonics do not

Classical technical analysis is the only family here with a real academic literature. The overall verdict is modest. Park and Irwin reviewed 95 modern studies and counted 56 positive, 20 negative and 19 mixed ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jecsur/v21y2007i4p786-826.html); [AgMAS](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/37487/files/AgMAS04_04.pdf)). They found profits in many speculative markets "at least until the early 1990s." They also found that most of those tests suffered from data snooping, rules chosen after the fact, and weak handling of risk and costs ([Farmdoc](https://farmdoc.illinois.edu/publications/the-profitability-of-technical-analysis-a-review)). Later work found that technical-rule profitability "strongly declined or even ceased to exist" in stocks, with similar declines in FX and futures ([WIFO](https://wifo.ac.at/wp-content/uploads/upload-4606/WP_2007_289.pdf)). Read the classical catalogue as context and risk-placement tools, not as validated stand-alone systems.

### Dow Theory gave trading its definition of trend

"Higher highs and higher lows" comes from Charles Dow's 1900–1902 *Wall Street Journal* editorials and William Peter Hamilton's application of them. The six tenets are standard textbook material:† the averages discount everything; there are primary, secondary and minor trends; primary trends have accumulation, public-participation and distribution phases; the averages must confirm each other; volume confirms the trend; and a trend persists until a definite reversal signal. A reversal requires a failure to make a new high, followed by a break of the prior trough.

This is one of the few classical ideas with a rigorous re-test. Brown, Goetzmann and Kumar (*Journal of Finance*, 1998) argued that Cowles' 1934 test, which seemed to discredit Hamilton, actually supports him once risk is adjusted for: Hamilton's timing earned positive risk-adjusted returns ([Yale SOM](https://viking.som.yale.edu/the-dow-theory-william-peter-hamiltons-track-record-re-considered)). Two further results come from secondary sources. Hamilton was right about short-term direction about 60% of the time ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=582)). A neural network trained to copy his calls beat buy-and-hold by about 4.4 percentage points a year from 1930 to 1997 ([NYU Stern](https://stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/news-events/prof-stephen-brown-s-research-dow-theory-featured)).

That evidence covers slow, index-level timing. It says nothing about "break of structure" on a 1-minute NQ chart. SMC's BOS/CHoCH vocabulary relabels Dow's swing logic. So do Victor Sperandeo's "1-2-3 trend change"† and his "2B" failed-new-high rule.† The 1-2-3 sequence is: trendline broken, retest fails to make a new extreme, prior swing broken. Alexander Elder's Triple Screen formalized multi-timeframe analysis with three timeframes roughly five times apart.† A typical FX or gold version uses daily bias, 4H setup and 15-minute trigger; an NQ/ES version uses 60-minute, 15-minute and 1–5-minute charts.† **No quantitative test of multi-timeframe alignment was found.**

### Support, resistance and round numbers have the best FX evidence

Carol Osler's New York Fed work is the strongest evidence that "levels" matter in FX. Her 2000 paper used published support and resistance levels from six firms between January 1996 and March 1998. She tested them against one-minute USD/DEM, USD/JPY and GBP/USD quotes. The levels helped predict intraday trend interruptions, but predictive power varied by firm and currency ([FRBNY](https://resources.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). The exact hit rates could not be retrieved this session.

Her order-book study explains why. Take-profit orders **cluster at round numbers**, so trends tend to stall there. Stop-loss orders **cluster just beyond round numbers**, so trends tend to accelerate once a level breaks ([FRBNY SR125](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr125.html)). Stop-loss execution also moves price more than take-profit execution does ([FRBNY SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.html)).

Gold shows the same psychology. Aggarwal and Lucey found that round-number barriers affect both the mean and the variance of gold returns near those levels ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/wly/revfec/v16y2007i2p217-230.html)). Lucey and O'Connor found gold fixed less often at prices ending in 0 and 00 ([UCC](https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/mind-the-gap-psychological-barriers-in-gold-and-silver-prices/)).

The practical reading is to treat the first touch of a big figure as a fade candidate and a clean push through as a momentum candidate. Big figures are 1.1000 and 1.1050 on EURUSD, $x,x00 and $x,x50 on gold, and 100- and 50-point handles on NQ/ES. This is the empirical core of the ICT "liquidity" story. Two caveats apply. The data is from 1996–98. And **no NQ/ES-specific round-number study was found.**

**Previous-day and previous-week highs and lows (PDH/PDL, PWH/PWL)** are traded the same way.† A sweep beyond the level followed by a reclaim is a reversal setup. Acceptance beyond it is a continuation setup. No academic test of these levels was found.

**Supply and demand zones** in Sam Seiden's Online Trading Academy style are another way to draw levels. A demand zone is the base before a rally: Rally-Base-Rally (continuation) or Drop-Base-Rally (reversal). A supply zone is the base before a drop: Drop-Base-Drop or Rally-Base-Drop. The OTA "odds enhancers" are the strength of the departure from the base, time spent at the base (less is better), freshness (untested zones are better), room to the opposing zone, trend, and position on the higher-timeframe "curve." Seiden calls buying the initial departure a "sucker bet." Traders wait for price to return to the zone ([El Trader Financiado](https://www.eltraderfinanciado.com/en/blog/supply-and-demand-zones)). No independent test of the odds enhancers exists.

**Pivot points** are formula-based levels from the prior session's high (H), low (L), close (C) and sometimes open (O). The table gives the textbook formulas, none of which was verified this session. Results depend on which session close is used: FX conventionally uses the 17:00 New York close, while ES/NQ traders split between the 09:30–16:00 regular session and the full Globex day.†

### Trendlines and wedges have rules but no supporting statistics

Trendline rules come from Edwards and Magee (1948) and John Murphy.† An uptrend line joins two or more rising swing lows, and a third touch confirms it; more touches and longer duration make a stronger line, and lines steeper than about 45° tend to break. A channel adds a parallel line through the intervening swing highs. In a break-and-retest, after a close beyond the line, enter on the pullback that holds the broken line from the other side. The stop goes beyond the retest swing. The target is the prior swing or a channel-width projection.

**Trendline breaks** are traded in two opposite ways. The classical approach enters on the break or the retest. The ICT/SMC "trendline liquidity" approach expects the line to be swept before price reverses.

The trendline-liquidity idea extends Osler's stop-clustering finding from round numbers to sloped lines. It assumes many traders draw the same line, which has not been tested. Trendlines are far less consensual than round numbers, so treat it as anecdotal.

Tom DeMark's tools are documented but untested.† **TD Lines** connect the two most recent "TD points," drawing right to left. A breakout qualifies if the prior bar closed against the breakout, the open gaps beyond the line, or a calculated value lies beyond it. A **TD Sequential setup** is nine consecutive closes below the close four bars earlier. The setup is "perfected" when bar 8 or 9 undercuts the lows of bars 6 and 7. The setup's high becomes TDST resistance, and the **countdown** then counts 13 bars, not necessarily consecutive, closing at or below the low two bars earlier.

No peer-reviewed evidence was found for TD Sequential on FX or futures.

The only hard data on wedges is unfavourable. In Thomas Bulkowski's bull-market table for upward breakouts, **rising wedges rank last of 36 patterns** for both performance and failure rate ([Bulkowski rank](https://www.thepatternsite.com/rank.html)). This suggests treating rising wedges as bearish. That matches Al Brooks' view of wedges as three-push reversal patterns.†

### Bulkowski's pattern statistics favour reversal bottoms over flags

Bulkowski's database is the largest public set of chart-pattern statistics. It is built on **US stocks, daily data, and "perfect trades"**: entry at the breakout and exit at the ultimate high or low.

Two definitions matter for reading the table. **Break-even failure** is the share of patterns that fail to move more than 5% beyond the breakout before reversing ([Failure Rates](https://thepatternsite.com/FailureRates.html)). **Meet target** is the share that reach the textbook measured-move target.

The figures are stock-based and do not transfer directly to FX, futures or gold. Magnitudes don't carry over: a 5% failure threshold and 40–50% average moves are calibrated to individual stocks. EURUSD's daily range is well under 1%. Failure rates are also rising: a study of about 14,000 patterns from 1991 to 2008 found that **failure rates have risen two- to fourfold since the 1990s**. The 10%-failure rate for upward breakouts went from 14% in the 1990s bull market to 28% in 2003–07 ([Failure Rates](https://thepatternsite.com/FailureRates.html)).

Two misreadings are common. The "95% success" quoted for cup-with-handle is a misreading of its 5% failure rate. The "83%" quoted for ascending triangles is a misreading of their 17% failure rate. On Adam & Eve bottoms, Bulkowski himself writes that the "chance of duplicating such a rise on a consistent basis is probably zero" ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/aedb.html)).

The academic tests are more encouraging, but they are old and mostly on equities. **Lo, Mamaysky and Wang (2000)** detected patterns automatically in US stocks from 1962 to 1996. They found "incremental information," especially in Nasdaq stocks ([MIT](https://web.mit.edu/~alo/www/Papers/techanal.html)). **Savin, Weller and Zvingelis** found that head-and-shoulders rules earned about **5–7% a year in risk-adjusted excess returns** in 1990s US stocks ([Savin et al.](https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/gsavin/papers/HSrevision_paw_10%2019%2006.pdf)). **Chang and Osler** tested head-and-shoulders on daily major-currency rates from 1973 to 1994 ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecj/econjl/v109y1999i458p636-61.html)). Osler later summarized the FX result this way: the patterns "were pretty useful," but "you could actually do a lot better using much simpler technical tools, such as moving averages or filter rules" ([S&C interview](https://traders.com/Documentation/FEEDbk_docs/2001/06/Abstracts_new/Interview/Interview.html)).

The **Quasimodo** (bearish case: high → low → higher high → lower low, then short the return to the left-shoulder level with a stop above the head) has no quantitative evidence at all.†

### Candlesticks fail as systems and survive only as triggers

The core academic test is Marshall, Young and Rose. They tested **28 candlestick signals on Dow Jones Industrial Average stocks from 1992 to 2002**, with 10-day holding periods. **No bullish signal reliably predicted abnormal positive returns.** One single-day bullish signal reliably predicted *negative* returns ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/candlesticks-fiddlesticks/); [Massey](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/items/edc6c1cf-63d9-4c94-a13d-92ffe1a53481/full)). A 2014 replication agreed ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/sin/wpaper/14-a010.html)).

Bulkowski finds a few rare multi-bar patterns with high reversal rates, but the samples are tiny. The bearish three-line strike reversed upward **84%** of the time, but he found only **85** examples in more than 4.7 million candle lines ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/ThreeLineStrikeBear.html)), and three stars in the south tops his reversal list at **86%** ([Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/CandleReverse.html)).

These tests reject candles as a mechanical system. They do not test candles used the way Steve Nison, Al Brooks and Nial Fuller use them: as entry triggers at levels that are already qualified. That use remains untested.

In 24-hour markets (FX, gold, and NQ/ES on Globex), the classic gap-dependent definitions are routinely relaxed. Examples are the morning star, the abandoned baby and the harami gap. Stock-based statistics therefore do not map one-to-one.

### The price-action schools share one skeleton and no audited record

The major discretionary price-action schools reduce to the same five steps: (1) define trend or range context, (2) wait for a pullback or test at a level, (3) enter on a stop beyond a signal bar or micro-range, (4) put the stop beyond the structure, and (5) target the prior swing or a measured move. None of the four schools below publishes audited records.

**Al Brooks** reads the ES 5-minute chart bar by bar. In a bull pullback, each bar whose high exceeds the prior bar's high is an attempt to resume the trend: the first is a High 1 (H1), the next a High 2 (H2), and a High 3 is a "wedge bull flag" variant. Bear rallies are counted the same way as L1/L2. Entry is a stop one tick beyond the signal bar, and the count resets when price breaks beyond the prior swing ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/bar-counting-h1-h2/); [Brooks forum](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/01-terminology/h1-h2-l1-l2-etc/paged/2/)). His other core concepts† are the "always in" direction (if you had to hold a position, which side would you hold?), second entries beating first entries, wedges as three-push reversals or flags, measured moves where leg 1 ≈ leg 2, and the traders' equation, probability × reward > (1 − probability) × risk. He also says most breakout attempts from trading ranges fail, often citing about 80%, which is his own claim.

**Nial Fuller** trades daily-chart Forex. His signals are pin bars and inside bars at key levels, in confluence with the 8/21 EMA zone.† His inside-bar rule: the whole inside bar must sit within the mother bar's range. Enter with a buy stop above the mother bar's high or a sell stop below its low, with the trend, preferably after a *close* beyond the level ([Comparic](https://comparic.pl/strategia-inside-bar-nial-fuller/)). His combinations are a pin bar followed by an inside bar, an "inside pin bar," and a "50% tweaked" entry that waits for a retrace to the middle of a large pin bar ([Comparic](https://comparic.pl/strategia-tradingowa-pin-bar-inside-bar-combo-nial-fuller/)).

**Bob Volman** scalps EUR/USD on a 70-tick chart. The only indicator he allows is an exponential moving average ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/13269524-forex-price-action-scalping)). He sorts breakouts into **false, tease and proper breaks**, and looks for "build-ups" of price pressed against a level ([TradingView](https://vn.tradingview.com/ideas/falsebreak/)). His named setups† are the double-doji break, first break, second break, block break, range break and inside range break.

**Lance Beggs (YTC)** teaches intraday swing structure across six volumes ([listing](https://globaltradingtools.com/?p=17029)). His setups† are the test of support or resistance (TST), breakout failure (BOF), breakout pullback (BPB), simple pullback (PB) and complex pullback (CPB).

The four schools fit different markets. Brooks transfers most naturally to ES and NQ, since he built his method on the ES 5-minute chart. Fuller's daily-chart approach is the least sensitive to trading costs. Volman's 70-tick EUR/USD scalping is the most cost-sensitive of all.

**Gaps** in classical taxonomy come in four types:† **common** gaps form inside ranges and fill quickly; **breakaway** gaps start a move and often stay open; **runaway (measuring)** gaps form mid-trend and often mark the halfway point; and **exhaustion** gaps come late in a trend and fill quickly, sometimes forming an island reversal.

This stock-market taxonomy maps poorly onto the near-24-hour markets covered here. For ES/NQ the tradable gap is the 9:30 a.m. ET open against the prior 4:00 p.m. close. For FX and gold it is mainly the weekend gap. The gap-fill statistics appear in the order-flow section below.

## Indicators mostly trim drawdowns rather than create profits

Indicator strategies fall into four groups: trend filters, momentum oscillators, volatility tools and volume measures. The pattern in the evidence is consistent. **Trend indicators mostly reduce drawdowns rather than add return.** Very short-term oscillators used for mean reversion on US equity indices show the best practitioner results. Standard settings used as stand-alone signals, such as RSI 70/30 or the MACD crossover, test poorly. Almost every performance figure in this section comes from practitioner or vendor backtests that do not state their costs, and each table flags this.

### Trend indicators: moving averages, Supertrend, SAR, ADX, Ichimoku, Donchian

The best-documented trend filter is the 50/200-day "golden cross" on the S&P 500. Tests find it roughly matches buy-and-hold returns while cutting the worst losses by about half. QuantifiedStrategies reports **6.8% vs. 7.2% CAGR** with **maximum drawdown of 33% vs. 56%**, invested about 70% of the time ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/moving-average-crossover-trading)). StockCharts reports 7.42% vs. 6.90% over 1990–2016 ([StockCharts](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-arthurhill-2016-06-trading-strategy---crossing-to-the-dark-side---quantifying-the-golden-cross-for-the-sp-500/)). The same author found the cross **did not beat buy-and-hold on the Dow or the Russell 2000** ([StockCharts](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-arthurhill-2016-06-testing-the-golden-cross-with-the-nasdaq-100-and-russell-2000---do-short-positions-add-value---pitfalls-of-optimization/)).

The academic picture explains why. Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992) found moving-average and range-break rules informative on the Dow Jones from 1897 to 1986 ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v47y1992i5p1731-64.html)). Correcting for data snooping changes that. Sullivan, Timmermann and White found the best rule on 1987–1996 data earned a **statistically insignificant 8.63% a year before costs** ([FMG/LSE](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-11/dp303.pdf)). A further re-test found simple rules significantly profitable on the **NASDAQ Composite and Russell 2000 but not the Dow or S&P 500** ([Hsu & Kuan](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/211/Re-Examining%20the%20Profitability%20of%20Technical%20Analysis%20with%20Whitets%20Reality%20Check%20and%20Hansen9s%20SPA%20Tes.pdf)). That predictability then **weakened after index ETFs were introduced** ([HKU](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/229640)).

Across markets, the strongest trend evidence is not intraday at all. AQR's century-long study of time-series momentum covered 1-, 3- and 12-month lookbacks in **67 markets**, including commodities, equity indices, bonds and currencies, from 1880 to 2016. It found **positive returns in every decade** and good performance in equity bear markets ([AQR via TrendFollowing.com](https://www.TrendFollowing.com/whitepaper/Century_Evidence_Trend_Following.pdf); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/trend-following-with-intrinsic-momentum-over-the-very-long-run)).

Gold shows the same split between slow and fast rules. A 12-month moving-average timing rule reportedly grew **$100,000 (1971) to about $10 million vs. about $4 million** for buy-and-hold, though costs were not shown ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/gold-moving-average-strategy-best)). A broader study found monthly gold MA timing outperformed and attributed this to investor underreaction ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/an-extensive-test-of-market-timing-strategies-in-the-gold-market/)). By contrast, conventional moving-average crossovers on **5-minute spot gold and silver from 2008 to 2015 were not profitable** ([Urquhart et al.](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/174/does_technical_analysis_beat_the_market__evidence_from_high_frequency_trading_in_gold_and_silver.pdf)). Dual-MA and channel rules on COMEX gold futures from 1990 to 1998 were **not significant** ([Bond University](https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/profitability-of-trading-rules-in-futures-markets/)). A test of **7,846 rules on 15 commodity futures** found none reliably profitable on its own ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/does-technical-trading-work-with-commodity-futures/)).

In Forex, moving-average and filter-rule profits from the 1970s and 1980s were genuine but **had disappeared by the early 1990s** ([Neely, Weller & Ulrich](https://gpbib.pmacs.upenn.edu/gp-html/Neely_2009_JFQA.html)). A data-snooping-controlled study of **21,000+ rules across 30 currencies over 45 years** still found predictability. It was strongest in emerging and less-mature currencies and varied over time ([Hsu, Taylor & Wang](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/technical-trading-is-it-still-beating-the-foreign-exchange-market/)). A major survey concluded that technical analysis is widespread among FX professionals and "may be profitable." It found the most plausible explanation to be signals about non-fundamental influences ([Menkhoff & Taylor](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/aeajeclit/v_3a45_3ay_3a2007_3ai_3a4_3ap_3a936-972.htm)).

The oscillator evidence splits cleanly by holding period. **Very short lookbacks** (RSI(2), 5-day %R, IBS) **used as mean-reversion entries within an uptrend** on US equity indices have the strongest practitioner support. That applies most directly to ES and NQ traded on daily bars. A paywalled source even reports an IBS portfolio across ES, NQ and YM futures ([Alpha Algo](https://algotr.substack.com/p/the-mean-reversion-portfolio-that)). No source shows these results transfer to EURUSD or gold, which have historically trended more. Because these systems trade often and hold for about two days, unstated costs matter a great deal.

**Divergence** is the most widely taught oscillator setup and has no rigorous performance evidence.† In a **regular** divergence (a reversal signal), price makes a lower low while the oscillator makes a higher low (bullish), or price makes a higher high while the oscillator makes a lower high (bearish). In a **hidden** divergence (a continuation signal), price makes a higher low while the oscillator makes a lower low (bullish), or the mirror (bearish). Cardwell's "positive reversal" is the same thing as hidden bullish divergence ([Traders.com](https://technical.traders.com/tradersonline/display.asp?art=4866)).

### Volatility and volume tools set stops better than they pick entries

**ATR (average true range)** is the backbone of modern stop placement and position sizing. True range is the largest of high − low, |high − prior close| and |low − prior close|; ATR(14) is its Wilder-smoothed average.† The **Chandelier Exit** hangs a long stop at the **highest high of 22 bars − 3 × ATR(22)** ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/equities/chandelier-exit/)). In one worked ES example, a high of 5268 with ATR 8.5 puts the stop at 5242.5 ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/indicators/chandelier-exit)). Supertrend, UT Bot and the Turtles' 2N stop all belong to the same family. ATR stops normalize risk across instruments as different as EURUSD, NQ and XAUUSD. This is the most transferable indicator idea in the catalogue, even where the entry signals do not transfer.

**Bollinger Bands** are a 20-period SMA ± 2 standard deviations. Two derived measures matter. **%b** = (close − lower band)/(upper − lower), so 0 is the lower band and 1 the upper. **BandWidth** = (upper − lower)/middle. A "squeeze" is commonly defined as BandWidth at its lowest in 125 periods ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/bandwidth.md)).

The bands support two opposite playbooks. In ranges, **fade** the touch: buy %b ≤ 0 and exit at the middle band. In trends, **follow** the squeeze breakout and accept that price can "walk the band." Fading a trending band walk is the classic losing trade. Bollinger himself says the bands "do not provide continuous advice; rather they help identify setups where the odds may be in your favor" ([BollingerBands.com](https://www.bollingerbands.com/bollinger-band-rules)). His Methods I–III cover volatility breakout, trend-following with %b and MFI, and W-bottom/M-top reversals.† **No net-of-cost evidence for either Bollinger playbook on ES, NQ, FX or gold was found.**

**Volume indicators** need real volume. On-Balance Volume (OBV) is a running total that adds volume on up closes and subtracts it on down closes.† Chaikin's Accumulation/Distribution line and Chaikin Money Flow weight volume by where the close falls in the bar's range.† The Money Flow Index (MFI) is a volume-weighted RSI read at 80/20.† VWAP is cumulative (typical price × volume) ÷ cumulative volume, reset each session.† Spot Forex and spot gold have no centralized volume. On MT4/MT5 or CFD charts these indicators run on broker **tick volume**, which counts price changes rather than contracts ([EarnForex](https://www.earnforex.com/guides/tick-volume-in-forex)). They are meaningful on CME futures (ES, NQ, GC, 6E) and weak proxies elsewhere.

### Popular TradingView tools and gold robots repackage old ideas

Today's popular TradingView scripts are mostly repackaged classics: **UT Bot Alerts** is an ATR trailing stop with a "Key Value" multiplier, a default ATR of 10 and an optional Heikin-Ashi mode ([TradingView](https://my.tradingview.com/script/lPLuynjG)). **LazyBear's Squeeze Momentum** is the TTM Squeeze with a linear-regression histogram. A vendor blog calls it the most-liked TradingView indicator at about 97,400 likes, which is unverified ([PickMyTrade](https://blog.pickmytrade.io/top-free-indicators-and-strategies-on-tradingview.md)). **"Smart Money Concepts" indicators** automate BOS/CHoCH, order-block and premium/discount labels.

None has an independent, net-of-cost track record.

Retail **gold expert advisors (EAs)** deserve a specific warning. "Gold trading EAs have flooded the market—most using dangerous grid/martingale tactics that eventually blow accounts when gold corrects" ([NYC Servers](https://newyorkcityservers.com/blog/ea-gold-stuff-review)). One EA specification escalates lots from **0.02 → 0.03 → 0.05 → 0.09 → 0.16 → 0.28 → 0.48**, which multiplies exposure about 24 times in seven steps ([MQL5 spec](https://c.mql5.com/21/597/EA_Specification_first_.txt?d=1)). Even a breakout (non-grid) gold EA showed a live **maximum drawdown of 41.66%**, far above its backtest; note that the reviewer may be an interested party ([NYC Servers](https://newyorkcityservers.com/blog/the-gold-reaper-review)).

A credible gold EA should pass four tests: at least 12 months of verified live trading on Myfxbook or FX Blue, no lot progression after losses (no martingale or averaging down), a hard stop on every trade, and live drawdown close to the backtest drawdown.

## Auction, order-flow and opening-range tools work best where volume is real

This family reads *where* trading happened and *who* was aggressive. It is the natural home of NQ/ES and gold futures, because CME products publish real exchange volume. Spot Forex and spot gold do not. The family splits three ways. Auction Market Theory and volume profile supply *location*. Footprint, delta and the order book supply *aggression*. VWAP, opening-range breakouts and intraday-momentum effects supply *timing*. NQ scalper Fabio Valentini, profiled later, describes his sequence as **"Direction, Location, Aggression"** ([TradeZella](https://www.tradezella.com/playbooks/auction-market-playbook)). Two parts of this family have the strongest quantitative evidence: the intraday-momentum results and the VWAP and opening-range-breakout (ORB) backtests. Both come with large caveats.

### Market Profile defines value, balance and the opening type

Market Profile was devised by J. Peter Steidlmayer, a Chicago Board of Trade trader. It was released publicly as a CBOT product in 1985 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_profile)). James Dalton's *Mind Over Markets* later systematized it into the auction reading traders use today ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/tpo-profile.md)).

Each 30-minute period gets a letter, and every price traded in that period prints the letter once. These prints are TPOs ("time price opportunities"). The row with the most prints is the **point of control (POC)**. The band holding about **70% of activity** is the **value area**, bounded by the value-area high (VAH) and low (VAL). The 70% figure is a convention borrowed from the normal distribution's ±1 standard deviation ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_profile)), and platforms differ (68% vs. 70%). The **initial balance (IB)** is the range of the first hour of regular trading, which is the first two TPO periods ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/initial-balance.md)).

**Volume Profile** applies the same idea to traded volume at each price instead of time ([El Trader Financiado](https://www.eltraderfinanciado.com/en/blog/volume-profile)). Three features matter. **High-volume nodes (HVNs)** are accepted prices where price slows and acts as a magnet. **Low-volume nodes (LVNs)** are prices that were crossed quickly; when revisited, price "often either accelerates through or gets rejected sharply." **Naked POCs** are untested prior-session POCs that serve as targets.

The profile can be drawn over different windows. A **composite** profile spans many days and shows the larger balance area. A **fixed-range** profile covers one swing and locates its internal LVNs. A **developing** profile updates live and shows whether value is migrating.

Three setups follow from these definitions. The first is an LVN rejection on a pullback in the trend direction, confirmed by order flow, with the stop beyond the LVN and the target at the next HVN or naked POC. The second is value-area rotation on balanced days. The third is a breakout from composite balance that is accepted outside it. Trader Dale is the best-known retail teacher of combining volume profile with order flow ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/pdf/order-flow-pdf-trader-dale)). **No independent test of HVN, LVN or naked-POC strategies on ES, NQ or GC was found.**

### VWAP trend-following has the strongest backtests, and weak replications

The session VWAP is the intraday fair-value reference for ES and NQ. ES's own daily settlement is a VWAP: specifically, the VWAP of all Globex trades from **14:59:30 to 15:00:00 Central Time** in the lead month ([CME](https://cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/faq-daily-settlement-price-determination-time-change.html)).

Brian Shannon popularized the **anchored VWAP**. It starts the calculation at a meaningful event, such as a swing low, a gap or the 8:30 data bar, to show who has controlled price since then. He calls it the "most accurate, objective measurement of supply and demand," buys strength after a dip once buyers regain the line, and places stops below the relevant low ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/general/education/22/05/27200039/my-favorite-ways-to-use-the-anchored-vwap-brian-shannon)).

The headline evidence is Zarattini and Aziz's VWAP trend rule: long above VWAP, short below. From January 2018 to September 2023 it turned **$25,000 into $192,656 net of commissions on QQQ (+671%)**, with a 9.4% maximum drawdown and a Sharpe ratio of 2.1. Buy-and-hold QQQ returned +126% with a 37% drawdown over the same period ([Concretum Group](https://concretumgroup.com/volume-weighted-average-price-vwap-the-holy-grail-for-day-trading-systems/)). A QuantConnect reproduction notes that public replications "reported far lower numbers" and concluded "there's no holy grail here" ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_0cab7e083e99d5a372e02fe882bbf078.html)).

The same team's Swiss Finance Institute working paper on SPY intraday momentum is more credible because it models costs. It trades breaks of a "noise area" around the day's open and uses dynamic trailing stops. From 2007 to early 2024 it reported **1,985% total return net of costs, 19.6% annualized, with a Sharpe ratio of 1.33**, and it modelled commissions and slippage ([Swiss Finance Institute](https://www.sfi.ch/fr/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)). The noise-area bands are the day's open ± the average absolute move from the open to that minute of the day over recent sessions ([Concretum Bands](https://it.tradingview.com/script/CUpWCZhe-Concretum-Bands)).

The opposite VWAP playbook is mean reversion: fade a move of about ±2 standard deviations from VWAP back toward VWAP. Practitioners say it works best on non-event days and in the lunch chop. It has no supporting backtest; one source guesses the unfiltered win rate is "closer to 45%" ([CrossTrade](https://crosstrade.io/learn/trading-strategies/vwap-reversion)). Every source agrees on the rule that matters: **classify the day first.** Use trend logic when price holds one side of a sloping VWAP, and reversion logic when price crosses a flat VWAP repeatedly. Misclassifying the day is how both playbooks lose ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/vwap-mean-reversion-vs-trend-regimes.md)).

| Exhaustion / failed auction | A new extreme printed on shrinking delta, with fading imbalances and thin volume at the extreme. | Fade back into the range. | [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/footprint-concepts/) |
| Heatmap / DOM liquidity (Bookmap) | Plots every order-book change. "Liquidity holding = potential support/resistance; liquidity pulling = potential breakout." | Trade with an order that keeps refreshing (iceberg-like). Expect a break when a large order is pulled. Detecting icebergs and spoofs needs market-by-order (MBO) data. | [Bookmap](https://bookmap.com/why-bookmap); [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/platforms/bookmap-cqg-futures-liquidity-trading) |
| Spoofing | Large displayed orders that are cancelled before they trade. | A reason to distrust displayed size, especially in gold. | See below |

Spoofing is the reason experienced order-flow traders distrust resting size. On 29 September 2020 the CFTC ordered **JPMorgan to pay $920.2 million** for at least eight years of spoofing in **precious-metals and Treasury futures**, involving "hundreds of thousands" of spoof orders. At the time it was the largest monetary relief in CFTC history ([CFTC](https://cftc.gov/node/233596)). Navinder Sarao spoofed in E-mini S&P futures and is linked to the 2010 Flash Crash ([Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/web/jpmorgans-big-hitters-of-gold-market-face-trial-over-spoofing/)).

The usual platforms are Sierra Chart, ATAS, NinjaTrader, Jigsaw (with its reconstructed tape) ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/vendors-product-reviews/26355-jigsaw-trading-s-peter-davies-ask-me-anything-ask-me-anything-4.html)), Bookmap, Quantower and DeepCharts. One forum user's impression is that London's Axia Futures teaches DOM "refresh/pull" reading around news and breakout momentum. That is a single opinion, not a documented method ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/traders-hideout/52641-orderflow-outdated-concept-9.html)). One TradingView script claims a "70–80% win rate" for footprint setups without evidence ([TradingView](https://de.tradingview.com/script/7S1vHl1Z-Advanced-Footprint-Analysis)). **No academic or independent evidence of order-flow profitability was found.** Note also that Tom Hougaard, often lumped in with order-flow traders, is a price-action and pattern trader. His book is about psychology ([CMC Markets](https://cmcmarkets.com/en-gb/opto/webinars/tricks-of-the-trade); [Harriman House](https://www.harriman-house.com/bestloserwins)).

### Opening-range breakouts show a real gross edge that costs can erase

Toby Crabel formalized the **opening range breakout (ORB)** in *Day Trading with Short Term Price Patterns and Opening Range Breakout* (1990) ([Oxford Strat](https://oxfordstrat.com/?p=8238)). The method places a buy stop and a sell stop a set "stretch" above and below the open ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/opening-range-breakout)). The stretch is commonly given as the 10-day average of the smaller of (high − open) and (open − low).† Crabel added **contraction filters**. An **inside day** has its high and low within the prior day's range; an **NR4** is narrower than each of the prior three days; an **NR7** is narrower than each of the prior six. He found ORB trades the day after an inside day or NR4 tended to coincide with trend days ([MarketInOut](https://www.marketinout.com/stock-screener/hub/toby-crabel)).

The modern evidence is mixed. Zarattini and Aziz's **5-minute ORB** works like this: if the first 5-minute bar of the session is up, buy at the start of the second bar; if it is down, sell; skip doji opens ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy)). On QQQ from 2016 to 2023, leveraged via TQQQ, it reported **+1,484% vs. +169% for buy-and-hold**, with 33% annualized alpha net of commissions ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622)). An independent replication found the paper's own **hit rate was only 24%, with +0.13R per trade**. It also found the paper modelled no spread or slippage and had no out-of-sample period. Run on index CFDs including Nasdaq from 2015 to 2026, **gross results matched the paper but net results were indistinguishable from zero, and four of five markets were negative** ([MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)).

The stronger version restricts the trade to **"Stocks in Play"**, i.e., stocks with abnormally high relative volume, usually on news. It reported **>1,600% net, a Sharpe ratio of 2.81 and 36% alpha** across 7,000+ US stocks from 2016 to 2023 ([University of St. Gallen](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/handle/20.500.14171/122125)). A QuantConnect replication on 1,000 stocks found a Sharpe ratio of about 2.4 ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)). For NQ and ES the lesson is that the gross edge per trade is thin, so execution costs decide the outcome. The 60-minute ORB is the same thing as the IB breakout.

**Intraday momentum is the most robust open/close effect.** Gao, Han, Li and Zhou (*Journal of Financial Economics*, 2018) found that **the first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return** in the S&P 500 ETF from 1993 to 2013. The effect is stronger on volatile, high-volume and major-news days ([WUSTL](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)). It is small, with a predictive R² of about 2% ([CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/first-and-last-half-hours-of-trading-linked)). Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens (*JFE*, 2021) extended it to **60+ futures markets from 1974 to 2020**, covering equities, bonds, commodities and currencies. They traced it to **gamma hedging** by options dealers and leveraged ETFs, and found it much stronger on days when dealers are net short gamma ([Erasmus](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-topic-does-gamma-hedging-actually-affect-stock-prices/)). This argues *against* blindly fading the day's move into the close.

**Gap fills on index futures** are mostly arithmetic. Scott Andrews mapped about 3,000 S&P futures gaps and sorted them by size relative to 40% of the 5-day ATR: smaller gaps fill far more often ([MoneyShow](https://moneyshow.com/articles/videotranstr-25181)). NexusFi reports, without naming its dataset, that small ES/NQ gaps of 0.25–0.5% filled the same day 84% of the time, with 48% filling within the first hour ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/fill-the-gap)). In SPY since 1993, 0.1–0.25% gaps filled the same day about 78–80% of the time, against only 26–33% for gaps above 1%. As that source notes, high fill rates on small gaps are "mostly arithmetic, not an edge by themselves" ([thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/gap-analysis)). The practical rule is to fade small gaps that open inside the prior range and treat large gaps beyond prior value with an open-drive as trend candidates.†

### Futures mechanics, options gamma and the gold clock shape intraday trading

ES and NQ trade on Globex from **Sunday 6 p.m. to Friday 5 p.m. ET**, with a daily 5–6 p.m. ET maintenance halt ([CME](https://cmegroup.com/markets/equities/sp/e-mini-sandp500.contractSpecs.html)). E-mini S&P settlement moved to **4:00 p.m. ET in October 2020** ([CME](https://cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/faq-daily-settlement-price-determination-time-change.html)). The NYSE publishes its regulatory **closing imbalance at 3:50 p.m. ET**. After 3:50 only offsetting market-on-close orders are accepted, and cancellations stop at 3:58 ([NYSE](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/NYSE_Auctions_Closing_Process_Fact_Sheet.pdf)). Practitioners say that imbalance moves ES and NQ into the close, but this has not been independently confirmed ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/market-structure/moc-imbalance-futures-trading)).

Zero-day-to-expiry (0DTE) options were **59% of SPX options volume in 2025**, "approaching 63%" by mid-2026 ([SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/0dte-options-explained/)). That has made dealer gamma a popular regime filter. Vendors map a "gamma flip" level, call and put walls, and a "volatility trigger." The rule of thumb is that positive dealer gamma stabilizes price, favouring VWAP and value-area reversion, while negative gamma amplifies moves, favouring ORB, IB breakouts and late-day momentum. The levels are proprietary and "do not predict direction" ([SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/gamma-exposure)). One academic study found higher 0DTE gamma associated with **lower** realized intraday volatility ([Harbourfront Quant summary of Dim, Eraker & Vilkov](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-0dte-options-cause-for)).

Gold's intraday clock has its own landmarks ([FXStreet](https://fxstreet.com/education/guides/gold/what-time-does-the-gold-market-open-gold-trading-hours)). Asian trading runs from about 18:00 to 03:00 GMT. London opens around 07:00 GMT, and the **LBMA auctions run at 10:30 and 15:00 London time**. COMEX/New York activity runs from about 13:30 GMT, and the London–New York overlap is described as the most liquid window. A thesis finds a "hat-shaped" gold pattern: prices rise in Asian hours and fall for the rest of the day ([CBS thesis](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/59803241/651553_Thesis_Contract_13383.pdf)). This is unreviewed work. Older COMEX evidence ranks US employment, GDP and CPI releases as the most market-moving announcements for gold ([J. Futures Markets](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v21y2001i3p257-278.html)).

### Intermarket filters confirm signals, and Forex lacks real volume

Intermarket comparisons are filters, not stand-alone signals. If NQ breaks out while ES, YM and RTY do not, that is either a sector rotation or a false breakout in progress ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/relative-strength-analysis-futures-day-trading)). In risk-off episodes, Treasury futures (ZN/ZB) rise while equities fall. A risk-off dollar surge often pushes gold lower, and VIX is strongly negatively correlated with ES ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/intermarket-analysis)). The practical version is to confirm an NQ breakout only when ES confirms too, and to treat a non-confirmation at a prior high or low as a fade candidate. For gold, the main inputs are the dollar index and real yields.

Spot Forex has **no central order book or consolidated volume**. Retail "volume" is tick volume, which differs from broker to broker ([EarnForex](https://www.earnforex.com/guides/tick-volume-in-forex)). It "cannot differentiate" a bank's block trade from a retail order, and it cannot show aggressor side, icebergs or absorption ([FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/balance-volume-forex-what-tick-volume-cannot-tell-you)). The standard workaround is to read **CME currency futures**, such as 6E for EUR/USD, which have a real order book, and then execute in spot ([FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/mastering-forex-order-flow-tools-setup-guide)). Scale is the caveat. CME FX futures and options traded about **$87–93 billion a day** in 2025 ([The Full FX](https://thefullfx.com/cme-highlights-options-new-fx-spot-peak-as-fx-volumes-drift/)). Global FX turnover is **$9.6 trillion a day** ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/media-releases/20250930-global-fx-trading-hits-96-trillion-day-april-2025-and-otc-interest-rate-derivatives-surge-79)). Futures delta therefore reflects a secondary venue. The same logic applies to gold: XAUUSD CFD traders should read GC/MGC futures order flow.

## Flow-driven timing and slow trend following own the strongest evidence

The best-evidenced effects in this catalogue share one feature. They follow from scheduled flows or slow behavioural drift, not from chart shapes. Examples are dealers rebalancing into FX fixes, options hedgers trading into the close, and investors underreacting over months. This section covers session timing, trend following, mean reversion, carry, news, macro and quantitative approaches, and the risk mathematics that decides whether any of them survives.

### Session strategies are well specified but mostly untested

Three peer-reviewed findings anchor this table.

**FX fixes.** Krohn, Mueller and Whelan (*Journal of Finance*, 2024) show the US dollar **appreciates into the Tokyo, ECB and London fixes and depreciates afterwards**. This traces a W-shaped intraday path across the top nine currencies over 21 years and implies daily swings worth more than **US$1 billion**. They attribute it to dealer inventory risk, and it shows up only intraday ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13306)).

**Local trading hours.** Breedon and Ranaldo found currencies tend to **depreciate during their own local trading hours**, driven by order flow ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/qmw/qmwecw/694.html)). A student thesis on EUR/SEK found no significant seasonality once volatility was filtered out, so the effects are not universal across pairs ([Lund University](https://lup.lub.lu.se/student-papers/record/9013814/file/9013817.pdf)).

**The old gold fix.** Caminschi and Heaney found that in gold futures and ETFs, **trades in the opening minutes of the old PM fix predicted its direction, in some cases more than 90% of the time**. They called this "highly suggestive" of information leakage ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v34y2014i11p1003-1039.html); [UWA](https://www.news.uwa.edu.au/201410067021/research/uwa-research-finds-gold-and-silver-price-setting-open-exploitation)). The UK Financial Conduct Authority fined Barclays about US$43.8 million in 2014 over gold-fix manipulation, and the fix was later replaced by electronic auctions ([UWA](https://researchimpact.uwa.edu.au/?p=2746)). That leak belongs to the old telephone fix and should not be assumed to exist in the current electronic auction.

### Trend following and momentum work slowly and across many markets

**Time-series momentum** is among the best-documented effects in all of finance. Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (*JFE*, 2012) tested 58 liquid futures and forwards and found **12-month time-series momentum returns positive for every contract examined**. Returns persisted for about a year and then partly reversed ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463)). The rule is simple. Each month, go long anything whose past-12-month excess return is positive and short anything negative. Size each position to a constant volatility target and hold for a month.† A blend of 1-, 3- and 12-month lookbacks showed a **1.8 gross Sharpe ratio** ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/intrinsic-momentum-diversified-across-futures/)). A counterpoint argues that per-asset predictability is weak and that pooled regressions overstate it (Huang et al., *JFE* 2020).†

**Cross-sectional currency momentum** means buying the recent winners and selling the losers among up to 48 currencies. It earned **up to 10% a year** from 1976 to 2010. The authors found "very effective limits to arbitrage" that keep these returns from being easily exploited ([Menkhoff, Sarno, Schmeling & Schrimpf](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeejfinec/v_3a106_3ay_3a2012_3ai_3a3_3ap_3a660-684.htm)).

The breakout version is the Donchian/Turtle template described in the indicator section. Holding periods run weeks to months, and many CTAs run it on gold, ES/NQ and currency futures. **The academic support is for slow, diversified, volatility-scaled trend following across dozens of markets.** That is a different activity from single-chart intraday "momentum" on NQ or XAUUSD, which these papers did not test.

### Mean reversion needs a known flow behind it

Rigorous mean-reversion evidence in these markets is thin. Classic pairs trading formed pairs from the closest-tracking stocks over 12 months and opened trades when the spread diverged by two standard deviations. The literature on it (Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst; Do & Faff) is commonly summarized as showing historical profits in US equities that declined sharply after the 1990s and were largely erased by costs; those figures were not verified this session.†

The **gold/silver ratio trade** goes long silver and short gold when the ratio is historically high, or the reverse when it is low, executed GC against SI hedge-ratio-weighted. One RSI(5) rule on the ratio **performed poorly on GLD**, and a pair variant was roughly flat ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/gold-silver-chart-ratio-strategy)). The **EURUSD vs. GBPUSD spread** is the same as trading EURGBP. It breaks down at regime shifts such as Brexit.

**Bollinger and RSI reversion on ES/NQ, FX or gold** rests on practitioner backtests only. The defensible version of mean reversion ties the fade to a known flow, such as a post-fix reversal or a liquidity-driven overshoot after news. Fading the day's trend into the close bets against the documented intraday-momentum effect.

### Carry pays steadily until it crashes

The FX **carry trade** goes long high-yielding currencies against low-yielders and collects the interest differential. A common retail version is long AUDJPY, MXNJPY or USDJPY. It works on average because the "forward premium puzzle" means high-yielders do not depreciate as much as theory predicts.† Its returns are **negatively skewed**: carry crashes when speculators unwind, especially as volatility spikes and funding dries up ([Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473)).

**The August 2024 yen-carry unwind** is the textbook recent example. The yen had weakened to about **¥162 per dollar** before snapping back about 10% in the three weeks after 11 July 2024 ([WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/gb/insights/blog/the-yen-carry-trade-unwinds-whats-next)). The Nikkei fell **12.4% on 5 August 2024**, its worst day since 1987 ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/markets/central-bank-body-says-carry-trade-crash-far-from-over/)). The Bank for International Settlements published the authoritative post-mortem ([BIS Bulletin 90](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull90.pdf)). Contributing triggers were a Bank of Japan hike on 31 July and a weak US payrolls report on 2 August.†

FX **value** (PPP reversion, measured as the negative five-year change in the real exchange rate) and **momentum** each earn premia. They are negatively correlated with each other, so combining them with carry diversifies better than any one alone (Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen, 2013).† A trader holding a single leveraged carry pair for swap income is, in effect, short volatility.

### News trading pits retail against the fastest machines

Prices adjust to macro **surprises** (actual minus consensus) within minutes (Andersen et al., 2003).† Intraday momentum is stronger on major news days ([Gao et al. via SUFE](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/356150)). The famous pre-FOMC drift, a documented tendency for US stocks to rise in the 24 hours before scheduled Fed announcements (Lucca & Moench), is reported to have largely disappeared after 2015; neither the size of the drift nor its disappearance was verified this session.†

Retail traders use three news tactics:

| Tactic | Mechanics | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **News straddle** | Place a buy stop above and a sell stop below the pre-release range shortly before NFP or CPI: about ±10–20 pips on EURUSD, ±$3–8 on gold, ±10–20 points on NQ. | Whipsaws that trigger both sides, spreads widening 5–20 times, and stop slippage. |
| **Fade the spike** | Wait 5–30 minutes for the first move to exhaust, then trade back toward the pre-release level. | Contradicts the evidence that markets reprice persistently to surprises. Valid only if the spike was liquidity-driven. |
| **"Second move"** | Enter the first pullback in the surprise direction. | The tactic most consistent with the evidence. |

The key calendar runs on US times:† non-farm payrolls (first Friday, 08:30 ET), CPI (mid-month, 08:30 ET), FOMC (eight a year, 14:00 statement and 14:30 press conference), ECB (eight a year, 14:15 CET), the BoJ (eight a year), and mega-cap earnings for NQ. **No rigorous study shows retail straddles or spike fades are profitable after costs.**

### Macro drivers: gold's central-bank bid, the dollar and the rates channel

Gold's 2024–2026 bull run reached an **all-time intraday high of $5,589.38 an ounce on 28 January 2026**. The LBMA PM price record was **$5,405** the next day. Both exceeded the inflation-adjusted 1980 peak ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/highest-gold-price-in-history-how-its-changed-from-2025-to-2026/)). Gold was still above $5,000 on 17 March 2026 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/gold-forecast-and-tracker-where-will-prices-land-in-2026-130000340.html)).

The World Gold Council's Q2 2026 report showed **central banks buying a net 289 tonnes in the quarter**, a second-quarter record. Even so, first-half official demand of 345 tonnes was the lowest since 2022, Russia was the largest seller, and **gold ETFs saw 45 tonnes of outflows** as prices cooled ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q2-2026)).

The classic gold model links it negatively to US real yields (Erb & Harvey).† That model would have predicted weakness in 2022–23. Instead gold rallied on official buying. A 2026 gold trader therefore needs to watch central-bank flows and geopolitics as well as yields.

In Forex the dollar sits on one side of **89%** of all trades ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/media-releases/20250930-global-fx-trading-hits-96-trillion-day-april-2025-and-otc-interest-rate-derivatives-surge-79)), so US rates and data drive almost every pair. Central-bank policy divergence drives the big trends, as with the 2022 surge in USDJPY.† Gold and NQ share a rates channel: a hot CPI print raises expected real yields, which typically strengthens the dollar and weighs on both gold and long-duration tech.†

Two positioning tools complete the macro kit. The CFTC's **Commitments of Traders** report comes out Fridays at 15:30 ET with positions as of Tuesday. Managed-money or leveraged-fund extremes are read as crowding signals. Research finds speculator positions forecast continuation while hedger positions are contrarian, and that FX speculator flows are contemporaneous rather than predictive.† Because the data is weekly and three days stale, COT is a swing tool. **Retail sentiment** indicators, such as IG Client Sentiment and Myfxbook's community outlook, are marketed as contrarian signals. They have no peer-reviewed out-of-sample test.†

### Quant, algorithmic and martingale systems fail on statistics before markets

Retail algorithmic trading faces the overfitting trap that academics have documented. Try enough strategy configurations and a high in-sample Sharpe ratio is almost guaranteed even when there is no true edge ([Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf)). Harvey and Liu propose discounting backtest Sharpe ratios to account for multiple testing, and a t-statistic hurdle near 3.0 rather than 2.0.† Walk-forward analysis (optimize on one window, test on the next, roll forward) reduces this bias but does not remove it if the whole process is repeated many times. Machine-learning gains in the literature are concentrated in large monthly cross-sections, not single-instrument intraday gold or FX (Gu, Kelly & Xiu).†

The mathematics of **martingale** systems, which double the stake after each loss, is unforgiving. After 10 straight losses a trader has lost 1,023 units and must stake 1,024 on the next trade. Streaks of that length are not rare:

| Chance of loss per trade | ≥10-loss streak within 250 trades | Within 1,000 trades | Within 5,000 trades |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | about 11% | about 39% | about 91% |
| 55% (a coin flip after costs) | about 24% | about 68% | about 99.7% |

These are the researchers' own calculations.

**Grid** systems add positions at fixed intervals against the move. They profit in ranges and pile up unbounded losing inventory in a persistent trend, which is exactly what gold's 2024–2026 run and policy-driven FX trends produced. Both designs produce smooth equity curves until a single streak wipes the account out: a short-volatility payoff like carry.

### Risk math decides survival, and prop rules push traders toward the worst evidence

**Trading styles** are defined by holding period. **Scalping** lasts seconds to minutes, and costs are a large share of each trade's expected move. **Day trading** closes everything by the session end. **Swing trading** holds for days to weeks and carries overnight gap risk. **Position trading** holds for weeks to months or longer; this is where carry, time-series momentum and macro live.

The risk frameworks themselves are pure mathematics (the researchers' own calculations):

| Framework | Formula | Worked numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-fractional risk | Risk a constant fraction *r* of current equity. Drawdown after *k* straight losses = 1 − (1 − *r*)ᵏ. | At 1% risk: 10 losses = 9.6%, 20 losses = 18.2%. At 2%: 18.3% and 33.2%. At 5%: 40.1% and 64.2%. |
| R-multiples and expectancy | R = initial risk. Expectancy = p × average win (in R) − (1 − p) × 1. | Break-even win rate = 1/(1 + b) at reward:risk b: **50% at 1:1, 40% at 1.5:1, 33.3% at 2:1, 25% at 3:1**, and 66.7% at 0.5:1. |
| Kelly criterion | f* = p − (1 − p)/b ([Kelly 1956](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1956.tb03809.x)). | p = 0.55 and b = 1 give 10%. p = 0.40 and b = 2 give 10%. **Half-Kelly keeps about 75% of the growth with half the volatility**, so practitioners bet ¼ to ½ Kelly. |

**Prop-firm rules strongly shape strategy.** Topstep's 2026 terms give a 90/10 profit split for new sign-ups and a $125 minimum payout. Payouts on its "Standard" Express path are capped at 50% of the account balance, up to $5,000 ([Topstep](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/8284233-topstep-payout-policy)). FTMO's standard two-step evaluation has profit targets of 10% and 5%, a 5% maximum daily loss and a 10% maximum total loss.† Check current terms on [FTMO](https://ftmo.com/en/trading-objectives/). Futures firms typically use trailing drawdowns and "consistency" rules that cap any one day's share of profits.†

The consequences follow from the risk table above. A 5% daily limit at 1% risk per trade allows only about five same-day losers. That penalizes low-win-rate trend systems that win 30–40% of the time at 2–3R. Trailing drawdowns punish giving back open profit, which works against letting winners run. Consistency rules punish the one-big-day results that news straddles and leveraged gold scalps produce.

**The rules therefore push traders toward intraday NQ, ES and gold scalping.** That is the style with the worst retail evidence, and it works against the slow, positive-skew trend following that has the best academic evidence.

### Forex rewards flow-awareness over pattern-hunting

Forex has the oldest and most rigorous technical-analysis literature. The verdict is that **simple rules once worked and then faded**. Moving-average and filter-rule profits on major pairs disappeared by the early 1990s ([Neely, Weller & Ulrich](https://gpbib.pmacs.upenn.edu/gp-html/Neely_2009_JFQA.html)). Two things still show up in rigorous work. The first is reactions at round numbers and published levels ([Osler](https://resources.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). The second is flows around benchmark fixes ([Krohn et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13306)).

That points a retail FX trader in three directions. Respect big figures, where take-profits stall price and stops sit just beyond. Know where the Tokyo, ECB and London fixes fall in the day. Treat ICT's London killzone and Judas swing as untested hypotheses built on the same stop-clustering mechanism. Order-flow methods need the CME 6E/6B proxy.

At the slow end of FX, carry, value and momentum are the academically supported factors. All three are best run as diversified portfolios rather than single-pair bets ([Menkhoff et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeejfinec/v_3a106_3ay_3a2012_3ai_3a3_3ap_3a660-684.htm)).

### NQ and ES reward session structure and real volume

Index futures have several features Forex lacks: a hard daily halt, a 9:30 cash open, a VWAP-based settlement, a closing auction and enormous options-hedging flows. These create the best-documented intraday effects for NQ and ES: first-half-hour → last-half-hour momentum ([Gao et al.](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)) and gamma-driven late-day momentum across 60+ futures ([Baltussen et al.](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/)). VWAP trend rules and opening-range breakouts show strong gross backtests on QQQ and SPY ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/volume-weighted-average-price-vwap-the-holy-grail-for-day-trading-systems/); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622)). An independent replication found **the ORB's net-of-cost edge on index CFDs was indistinguishable from zero** ([MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)).

NQ is the main stage for ICT, TJR and Venom, and for most prop traders. Its higher volatility means stops measured in points are wider than on ES. SMT between NQ and ES is the community's favourite confirmation, but correlations shift from year to year, so the comparison weakens when they do ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/data/intermarket-correlation-data)). Four-time World Cup champion Andrea Unger reports matching **mean-reversion** rather than trend logic to the E-mini S&P ([The Algorithmic Advantage](https://thealgorithmicadvantage.substack.com/p/038-andrea-unger-672-returns-sure)).

### Gold rewards patience at long horizons and punishes leverage at short ones

Gold is the hardest of the three to trade intraday. It trends hard, its record run took it from under $5,100 to $5,589 within days in January 2026 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/26/gold-record-surges-past-new-5000-record.html); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/highest-gold-price-in-history-how-its-changed-from-2025-to-2026/)), and conventional intraday MA rules tested unprofitable ([Urquhart et al.](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/174/does_technical_analysis_beat_the_market__evidence_from_high_frequency_trading_in_gold_and_silver.pdf)). The tools with real support work at long horizons: monthly MA timing ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/an-extensive-test-of-market-timing-strategies-in-the-gold-market/)) and gold as a trend-following constituent. Round-number barriers are documented in gold specifically ([Aggarwal & Lucey](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/wly/revfec/v16y2007i2p217-230.html)).

Gold's speed is also why prop-challenge traders favour it, and why grid and martingale EAs blow up on it. FTMO's own interviews describe successful gold traders who wait **15–30 minutes after the London open** before the first trade and risk small amounts at high reward-to-risk ([FTMO](https://ftmo.com/en/blog/interviews-with-new-ftmo-traders/)). That is the opposite of the "gold scalping bot" marketing.

## No verified winner trades ICT; the record-holders trade trends, macro and systems

The question was which strategies the biggest and most profitable traders use. Answering it means separating three groups. The first is **legends** whose records come from Schwager interviews and the press (grade B). The second is **competition champions** whose results come from real-money, audited contests (grade A for titles). The third is **social-media educators** whose results are almost entirely self-reported (grade C or D). The pattern is stark. **Among traders with verified or semi-verified records, no one is documented trading ICT or SMC.** Their edges cluster in four places:

| Edge | Traders |
|---|---|
| Macro direction with simple technical timing | Jones, Soros, Druckenmiller, Kovner, Lipschutz, Krieger |
| Systematic trend following | Seykota, the Turtles, Parker |
| Short-term breakout and mean-reversion systems in futures | Larry Williams, Raschke, Unger, Scherman |
| Classical chart patterns | Brandt |

The common thread is risk control rather than entry method. These traders use asymmetric reward:risk, size positions by risk or volatility, and exit fast when wrong. Among the modern traders, only one even claims an audited contest result: Fabio Valentini, an NQ order-flow scalper.

### Macro legends traded conviction and timed it with simple technicals

**Paul Tudor Jones** (Tudor Investment Corp.) trades stock-index, bond, FX and commodity futures on a horizon of days to months. His fund was heavily short going into the 1987 crash. Wikipedia credits the fund with **125.9% after fees in 1987** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tudor_Jones)), while accounts citing Schwager give about 62% ([Habrador](https://blog.habrador.com/2012/09/how-could-paul-tudor-jones-predict.html)). His rules are the most transferable in this set. He uses a **5:1 reward:risk** ("I could be wrong 80% of the time, and I'm still not going to lose"). He calls the **200-day moving average** his "metric for everything," and he uses it as an exit: "you get out. You play defense" ([25iq](https://25iq.com/2015/07/25/a-dozen-things-ive-learned-from-paul-tudor-jones-about-investing-and-trading)). His colleague Peter Borish's 1929 analog anticipated the 1987 crash ([Felder Report](https://thefelderreport.com/2014/03/13/for-everyone-who-thinks-tom-demarks-1929-analog-is-a-joke)). His use of Elliott Wave is commonly reported but was not confirmed here.† Strategy family: macro, timed with an indicator (the 200-DMA). Grade B/C, with conflicting figures.

**George Soros and Stanley Druckenmiller** (Quantum Fund; Druckenmiller also ran Duquesne) traded FX, bonds and equity indices on a horizon of weeks to months. Their 1992 short of sterling reputedly made **more than $1 billion**; one account puts the position at $10 billion. Druckenmiller is reported to have averaged **about 30% a year with no losing year** over about 30 years at Duquesne ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Druckenmiller)). That figure is self-reported and unaudited. Sources disagree on assets at the 2010 closure ($12 billion vs. $23 billion) ([elEconomista](https://www.eleconomista.es/mercados-cotizaciones/amp/2385804/Duquesnes-Druckenmiller-retiring-after-30-years)). Soros's theory of reflexivity and Quantum's roughly 30% long-run return are background claims.† Strategy family: discretionary macro with concentrated, conviction-weighted sizing. Grade B/C.

**Bruce Kovner** (Caxton Associates) traded FX, rates and commodities, with weeks-to-months macro positions. Caxton reportedly made about **$3 billion in its first ten years, at 55.6% a year gross**. A broker article is the only source, so treat this as grade C/D ([Dukascopy](https://www.dukascopy.com/swiss/english/marketwatch/articles/top-10-most-successful-forex-traders-in-the-world/)). His first trade was soybeans, funded with $3,000 on a credit card. His own anniversary note gives the most useful rule here: Caxton chose to **"target risk levels, not nominal dollar levels"** and allocated risk dynamically ([Caxton 20th Anniversary](https://brucekovner.com/Caxton20thAnniversary.html)).

**Michael Marcus** (Commodities Corporation) traded discretionary commodity and currency futures. He reportedly turned **$30,000 into $80 million**, about a 2,500-fold gain over ten years per Schwager ([TradeThePool](https://tradethepool.com/trading-books/market-wizards-book-jack-schwager/?read=1)). He learned from Ed Seykota and Amos Hostetter and mentored Kovner ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Marcus_(trader))). Grade B.

**Bill Lipschutz** is a pure FX specialist. He ran Salomon Brothers' proprietary FX account from 1984 to 1990 and founded Hathersage Capital in 1995 to trade **only G10 currencies** ([finnotes](https://finnotes.org/people/bill-lipschutz)). The "$300 million a year" figure attached to his Salomon years conflicts on year and on whether it was annual ([Wikipedia mirror](https://wikipedia2007.classicistranieri.com/en/b/i/l/Bill_Lipschutz_7245.html)). Grade C/D.

**Andrew Krieger** traded FX and FX options at Bankers Trust. After the 1987 crash he used currency options to short the New Zealand dollar in a position worth hundreds of millions, and the kiwi reportedly fell 3–5% ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/2020-11-26-the-biggest-forex-trades-ever-made)). A roughly $300 million profit figure appears in a single broker article ([Dukascopy](https://www.dukascopy.com/swiss/english/marketwatch/articles/top-10-most-successful-forex-traders-in-the-world/)). Grade D.

### Trend followers sized by volatility and held for months

**Ed Seykota** ran a systematic trend-following system across diversified futures. Schwager reports he grew one client account **from $5,000 to $15 million in 12 years** ([TurtleTrader](https://www.turtletrader.com/trader-seykota)). The often-quoted "250,000%" was not found in any source. His first system used exponential moving averages as a successor to Donchian channels. He ran it nightly, held positions for weeks to months and sized them by volatility. He is credited with the first commercial computerized trading system for client futures money ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Seykota); [DayTrading.com](https://www.daytrading.com/ed-seykota)). Grade B/D.

**Richard Dennis and the Turtles** traded breakout trend following on diversified futures, including currencies, gold and indices. The experiment tested whether trading can be taught ([CME Group](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/the-turtle-traders-featuring-richard-dennis)). The Turtles reportedly earned **$175 million** in aggregate. One former Turtle says this was over four years at about 80% compounded ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis); [The Short Bear](https://theshortbear.substack.com/p/richard-dennis)). The cautionary note matters most: backtests show the published Turtle rules suffered a **"drastic drop in performance after 1986" and were roughly flat from 1996 to 2009** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis)). Grade C/D. **Jerry Parker**, a 25-year-old accountant when he answered Dennis's advertisement, founded Chesapeake Capital in 1988 to run Turtle-derived long-term trend following ([TurtleTrader](https://www.turtletrader.com/?p=967)). Grade C.

### Competition champions use systems and short-term patterns, at extreme leverage

The **World Cup Trading Championships** (the "Robbins Cup") is the main real-money futures contest. Entrants fund real accounts and are ranked on **audited percentage return** ([Trading Tournaments](https://trading-tournaments.com/tournaments/championships/wctc)). Critics note the format rewards maximum profit while holding through deep drawdowns ([Gist summary](https://gist.ly/youtube-summarizer/the-truth-behind-robins-world-cup-trading-championship)). The official standings site could not be opened this session ([WCTC](https://www.worldcupchampionships.com/)), and the unofficial lists contradict one another.

**Larry Williams** won the 1987 World Cup with **11,376%** ([Trading Tournaments](https://trading-tournaments.com/articles/wctc-champions-year-by-year); [CMT Association](https://cmtassociation.org/?p=24881)). The result was checked twice before being accepted, and critics say no account statements were ever released publicly ([Comparic](https://comparic.pl/slawni-traderzy-larry-williams/)). His signature method is the **volatility breakout**, which uses the previous day's range as the entry trigger ([EBC](https://www.ebc.com/ru/forex/301635.html)). His other tools are reported but were not verified here:† Williams %R, seasonals, COT positioning, the "Oops" gap reversal and bond/gold filters. Grade A−, disputed. His daughter **Michelle Williams** won in 1997 with about **1,000%**, at age 16 or 17, coached by him ([StockCharts](https://c.stockcharts.com/articles/chartwatchers/2023/03/trading-legend-larry-williams-118.html)). Grade A.

**Andrea Unger** of Italy is the only four-time champion: **2008 (672%), 2009, 2010 and 2012** ([fxstreet.cz](https://www.fxstreet.cz/tym-purple-trading-andrea-unger-svetovy-sampion-v-tradingu.html); [Trading Tournaments](https://trading-tournaments.com/articles/andrea-unger-four-time-champion)). He trades **systematic futures**. He starts from simple, robust breakout or reversal ideas, adds context rules, and **matches strategy type to market character**: trend following on crude oil, and **mean reversion on the E-mini S&P**. He warns that competition returns come from "specifically trying to win the comp, and taking on the associated risks" ([The Algorithmic Advantage](https://thealgorithmicadvantage.substack.com/p/038-andrea-unger-672-returns-sure)). Grade A for the titles.

**Ivan Scherman** (CMT, CFTe) of Argentina won the **2023** Futures championship. Both the CMT Association and the WCTC's own X account confirm the title ([CMT Association](https://cmtassociation.org/technically_speaking/technically-speaking-july-2024/); [WCTC on X](https://x.com/WorldCupTraders/status/1755352059640242229)). He trades algorithmic, multi-asset strategies across currencies, energies, grains, indices, meats, metals including gold, softs and Treasuries, built with data mining and Python ([Medium](https://medium.com/@josue.monte/how-ivan-scherman-conquered-the-2023-world-cup-championship-with-data-science-dcdeebd6adc4)). His maximum drawdown during the contest was **26%**. The widely quoted **491%** return comes from unofficial and promotional sites; one gives $241,360 growing to $1,428,728 ([World Cup Advisor](https://www.worldcupadvisor.com/follow-ivan-scherman/)). Grade A for the title, C for the return.

### Chart and price-action specialists have the longest public careers

**Peter Brandt** (Factor Trading, founded 1980) trades classical chart patterns on futures, including COMEX gold and FX, holding for weeks to months ([PeterLBrandt.com](https://www.peterlbrandt.com/about-us/); [gold reports](https://www.peterlbrandt.com/premium-reports/page/41)). His record has three versions. Schwager's *Unknown Market Wizards* cites **58% compounded annually** over 27 years, which Brandt himself calls "inflated" because he traded his account aggressively ([Perlego](https://www.perlego.com/book/1990325/unknown-market-wizards-the-best-traders-youve-never-heard-of-pdf)). Benzinga reports 41% "at last audit" ([Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/9572095)). Factor's marketing says 40%. His commonly cited rules are risking ≤1% per trade, a "last-day rule" stop, and an expected 30–40% win rate with large winners.† Grade B, and B/C for the audit figure.

**Linda Bradford Raschke** trades short-term swing and day setups in futures. She began as an options market maker in 1981, registered as a CTA in 1992, and launched a hedge fund in 2002. Her bio says BarclaysHedge ranked it 17th of 4,500 for five-year performance, a self-reported claim. She retired as a CTA in 2015 ([LindaRaschke.net](https://lindaraschke.net/bio-linda/)). Her *Street Smarts* setups with Laurence Connors are **Turtle Soup** (fade a failed breakout of a prior N-day high or low), **80-20** (intraday) and **Holy Grail** (an ADX-confirmed pullback in a trend) ([TurtleTrader](https://www.turtletrader.com/trader-raschke/)). Turtle Soup is the documented, decades-old ancestor of the ICT/SMC "liquidity sweep." Grade C.

**Tom Hougaard** day trades DAX, FTSE and Dow index CFDs using price action. He is not an order-flow trader ([CMC Markets](https://cmcmarkets.com/en-gb/opto/webinars/tricks-of-the-trade)). His publisher says he risks up to £3,500 per point ([Harriman House](https://www.harriman-house.com/bestloserwins)). He claims to have turned **£25,000 into more than £1 million** in a year ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/60872327-best-loser-wins)), but his promoted Excel track record reportedly stops on 22 November 2021 ([Kagels Trading](https://www.kagels-trading.de/tom-hougaard-tradertom/)). Self-reported.

A fan-run tracker followed his 2024 entry ([ict-tracker](https://ict-tracker.onrender.com/stats)). **No official standing, statement or documentary was found.** All accessible sources place the episode in **2024, not 2023.** The fair summary is: widely reported as blown or abandoned, not officially confirmed, and the promised statements were never published. **TJR (Tyler J. Riches)** trades NQ/ES with an ICT-derived New York-open sweep model. His marketing includes a "$1,000,000/Month Trading Empire" video and a "7-figure" profile. ## Seven in ten retail accounts lose, and prop funnels pay about one buyer in fifteen

Every strategy in this catalogue is ultimately traded by people, and the hard data on people is consistent and bleak. It also explains why so many educators sell courses: the business model rewards marketing far more reliably than trading.

### Retail loss rates stay between 70% and 97% across countries and decades

European regulators publish the clearest figures. ESMA's 2018 review of national regulators' data found that **74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose money**, with average losses per client between €1,600 and €29,000 ([ZeroToInvest](https://zerotoinvest.com/en/reality-check/cfd-verlustquoten/); [CMS Law](https://cms.law/en/int/regulatory-news/esma-fca-measures-on-the-provision-of-contracts-for-differences-and-binary-options-to-retail-investors-in-the-eu)). ESMA then restricted the products ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-agrees-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-protect-retail-investors)):†

| ESMA 2018 measure† | Detail |
|---|---|
| Leverage caps | **30:1 on major FX; 20:1 on non-major FX, gold and major indices** |
| Margin close-out | Positions closed at 50% margin |
| Negative-balance protection | Clients cannot lose more than their deposit |
| Mandatory risk warning | Each broker must state its own percentage of losing accounts |

Those mandatory warnings now produce a running statistic. In April 2026, across **14 UK-authorized CFD brokers, the mean disclosed loss rate was 69.9%** and the median 71.0%; CMC Markets UK and IG UK each disclosed 68% ([The Investors Centre](https://www.theinvestorscentre.co.uk/trading/statistics/cfd-trading/)). Leverage caps appear to have reduced the size of losses more than the share of losing accounts, though no before-and-after data on loss size was retrieved.

Two large academic studies of day traders point the same way:

| Study | Sample | Key findings | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brazil: Chague, De Losso and Giovannetti | **All 19,646 individuals** who began day trading Brazilian mini-index futures in 2013–2015 | **97% of those who persisted beyond 300 days lost money.** Well under 1% earned more than a bank teller's starting salary. The best trader made about US$310 a day. There was **no evidence of learning**. The authors caution the results may not generalize. | [FGV](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b/full); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/day-trading-a-bust/) |
| Taiwan: Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean | Day traders, 1992–2006 | Unprofitable traders are more likely to quit, yet **74% of day-trading volume comes from traders with a history of losses**, and **97% of day traders are likely to lose money** in future day trading. This fits overconfidence, not rational learning. | [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rasset/v10y2020i1p61-93..html) |

A related paper by the same team is widely cited for finding that fewer than 1% of day traders predictably earn positive abnormal returns after fees, but that figure could not be confirmed this session ([Odean PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)).† US futures brokers are not required to publish loss rates the way EU and UK CFD brokers are.

### Prop-firm funnels pass about one attempt in six and pay about one buyer in fifteen

Topstep publishes the most transparent prop-firm data. For 2025 it reported four separate figures ([Topstep Disclosures](https://www.topstep.com/disclosures-notices)):

| Topstep 2025 measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Trading Combines (evaluations) completed and passed | **16.8%** |
| Individuals who reached the funded level at least once, across repeat attempts | **51.8%** |
| Funded-level individuals who received any payout | **33.3%** |
| Express-funded individuals moved to a live funded account | **0.71%** |

Topstep notes that its statistics mix simulated and live trading. Each figure has a different denominator, so they should not be multiplied together. Topstep also markets a 99.26% approval rate for payout *requests*, which measures something different from the share of traders who get paid.

A cross-firm dataset of about **300,000 accounts** from FPFX Technologies shows roughly **14% of evaluations passed and about 7% of all challenge buyers ever received a payout**. This comes via aggregators; the primary release was not accessed ([QuantVPS](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/prop-firm-statistics); [PropFirmsCompare](https://propfirmscompare.com/how-many-traders-pass-prop-firm-challenges/)). FTMO publishes no pass rate. The circulating "~10%" figure is an estimate ([Traders Second Brain](https://traderssecondbrain.com/guides/prop-firm-pass-rate)).

The economics explain the funnel. FTMO's parent reported **2024 revenue of about $329 million, with net profit above $62 million** ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/ftmos-parent-netted-over-62-million-on-329m-revenue-in-2024/)). Its 2025 revenue reached CZK 8.9 billion on about **1.27 million paid challenge orders** ([Around Prague](https://aroundprague.cz/en/people/ftmo-of-marek-vasicek-and-otakar-suffner-8-8bn-crowns-in-revenue-and-the)). That compares with **more than $450 million paid out over ten years** in total ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/ftmo-announces-over-450-million-paid-out-as-prop-trading-firm-turns-10/)). In December 2025 FTMO closed its roughly $422 million purchase of the broker OANDA ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/ftmo-paid-422-million-for-oanda/)).

A comparison of challenge fees with payouts suggests challenge fees, not trading profits, are the main revenue source. The industry is also fragile. An estimated **80–100 prop firms closed between January 2024 and early 2026**; True Forex Funds, for example, shut in May 2024 owing about 300 traders $1.2 million ([Medium, citing Finance Magnates estimates](https://medium.com/@veritaschain/what-the-80-firm-collapse-revealed-why-prop-trading-now-requires-a-technical-integrity-layer-d62983893cd8)).

For anyone evaluating an influencer's "funded" content, the implication is direct. With about one combine in six passed, one funded trader in three ever paid, and payouts copyable across several accounts, **payout screenshots are survivorship-filtered by construction**. **No firm publishes pass rates by strategy.** Any claim that "ICT/CRT/TJR passes more challenges" is anecdote or marketing.

## Conclusion

The most useful finding is that today's retail strategies are not as new as their names suggest. The "liquidity sweep" at the centre of ICT, SMC, CRT and TJR's model already existed in four older forms: Osler's evidence that stops cluster just beyond obvious levels, Raschke's 1995 Turtle Soup, Wyckoff's spring, and Sperandeo's 2B. The modern brands add named arrays, killzone minutes, macros and quarterly cycles, and that added layer is exactly the part nobody has tested. The useful question for a trader is therefore not which model is right. It is whether any single, mechanically defined version shows an edge after costs on out-of-sample data. An example would be "a PDH sweep, then a 5-minute FVG, on NQ between 9:30 and 11:00." Because definitions vary from educator to educator, each trader has to run that test personally, and Park and Irwin's warnings about data snooping apply in full.

The evidence-backed edges are either slow and diversified (time-series momentum, carry, value) or tied to scheduled flows (fixes, the close, options hedging, round-number order clustering). That points a trader away from the intraday scalping that prop-firm rules reward. The traders who verifiably made fortunes either managed capital with macro or trend-following discipline or won audited contests with systems. Today's most visible educators earn mainly from education, and their own public tests (the 2024 Robbins entry, a reported break-even admission, a SIM-account allegation) produced no verified profit. Trust evidence in this order: audited contest results, then regulator filings, then firm-published base rates. Payout screenshots, P&L screenshots and livestreams come last. Two practical rules follow: risk 1% or less per trade, and verify before you pay.
